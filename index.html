HOW THE MODEL WORKS

  • Salary gets the early-career raise every year of the early stage, then the raise steps down evenly until the late stage begins, and stays at the late-career raise after that. Spending and room rent rise with inflation. The installment is fixed in euros (fixed rate).
  • The car and wedding funds are cash that earns nothing, filled by their lines in the Stage 1 budget. Each line is saved until its date; when the first goal is paid, the share you choose of its amount moves to the other goal and the rest joins your leftover salary. Once both are paid, that money is free for your other spending and investing. If it falls short, the gap comes out of the deposit savings (before the house) or the portfolio (after). Car saving and running costs reduce the salary left to invest.
  • After the loan ends, the installment drops to zero, so the leftover salary, and what you invest from it, jumps. Each career stage uses its own investing share.
  • Stage 3 starts the month Stage 1 says the deposit is ready. Anything invested before then (Stage 1 slider) carries in as the starting portfolio.
  • Wedding gifts don't reduce what the wedding fund must hold. They arrive with the wedding and either pay down the loan (if you already have it) or go into the ETF. Paying down the loan keeps the installment the same, so the loan ends sooner.
  • Monthly income is averaged as 14 ÷ 12 salaries during the loan; the deposit stage uses the real payout months.
  • "Free" means portfolio value minus tax on its gains is at least the remaining loan balance. Greek banks generally allow early repayment of housing loans; confirm any fee with yours.
  • The S&P 500 return is a smooth average. Real markets fall 30–50% at times, so a crossover can move by years in either direction. Returns are in USD, so the EUR/USD rate also matters.
  • This is a planning tool, not financial advice. Check tax and lending rules with an accountant and your bank before deciding.
